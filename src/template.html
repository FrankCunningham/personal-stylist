Your personal stylist

Every color on this page was checked against the exact codes on your palette card. I sampled the pixels of each piece's official swatch or product photo, measured how far it sits from the nearest of your 18 colors, and kept only true matches. Every outfit is built with the 60-30-10 rule and a designer color formula. Every brand is in, whatever the price, as long as it comes in your size.

How the colors were checked

Pixels, not color names. Each color comes from the store's own fabric swatch (DXL, UNIQLO) or its product photo (True Classic, Good Man Brand, Perry Ellis, IZOD, G.H. Bass, American Giant, BYLT, Faherty, Thursday Boot Co., Toad&Co, tentree). For photos, the background is masked out and the garment's main color is found by clustering the pixels on the chest or legs. Black items were left out even when they read as charcoal, because a black shirt isn't charcoal in person.

Reference colors are your card's codes. I re-sampled the swatches in your screenshot and they match the printed codes, including the two the screenshot cuts off: Dark teal-navy #1F3A4D and Charcoal #374550. Every chip below shows the piece's measured code next to the card's code, so you can compare them yourself.

No fabric photo lands on the exact same code. Lighting, weave, and dye shift every pixel, and the same shirt photographed twice gives two codes. So "the same" here means measurably closest: use Color match in the builder to keep only near-exact (ΔE ≤ 3) or very close (ΔE ≤ 5) pieces.

ΔE is the distance to your card. It's the CIEDE2000 color difference between the piece and the nearest of your 18 colors. Anything above 8 was cut, and white or off-white had to be within 6 of cream or warm ivory to make it.

ΔE 0–3Near-exact. Side by side, most people can't tell them apart.
ΔE 3–6Very close. Same color, slightly lighter, darker, or softer.
ΔE 6–8Close. Clearly the same color family, and still on palette.

Honest limits. Photos carry studio lighting and heathered fabrics average out, so treat ΔE as a ranking, not a lab test. Stock and links are re-checked automatically every week (last check: ). Sold-out colors and dead links are hidden until they come back.

The design framework

  • 60-30-10
    About 60% of what you wear is a base neutral (espresso, dark teal-navy, charcoal, camel, or cream) on the structural pieces: pants, coats, jackets. About 30% is a supporting earthy color, like a forest green jacket or rust knit. About 10% is an accent jewel tone (burnt orange, teal, burgundy, or deep turquoise), usually the shirt peeking out of an open layer, or a scarf or tie. The bar under every outfit shows its real split.
  • Formulas
    Every outfit follows a designer formula: tonal (shades of one family), analogous (neighbors on the color wheel), or complementary (warm earth against cool jewel tones). If none fits exactly, the outfit still has to pass Classic 60-30-10.
  • Near the face
    No cool whites or cool grays by your face. Those spots go to cream, warm ivory, or camel.
  • Grounding
    Bold accents like burnt orange, teal, and deep turquoise always sit against a dark neutral such as espresso or dark teal-navy.
  • Texture
    Rich colors go on different fabrics (knit, canvas, twill, wool, denim) so they contrast instead of clash.
  • Proportion
    An open layer draws two vertical lines down your front, and darker on top balances a broad frame. Every piece sits within one step of formality of the others, and pants are straight, never skinny.

International brands

Japan: UNIQLO is in. Its US site sells men's tops up to 3XL, and every UNIQLO color on this list had 3XL in stock on Oct 25. UNIQLO's fabric-swatch photos were color-picked like DXL's.

Spain: none fit yet. Zara's largest men's size is a 2XL cut for a 44–46 inch chest. Mango Man stops at XXL, and I couldn't confirm anything above XXL at Massimo Dutti. El Corte Inglés has a big-sizes section, but sending it to the US means a quoted box-shipping service, not normal online delivery. None of them would fit a 260-pound, broad build reliably, so they aren't on the list.
